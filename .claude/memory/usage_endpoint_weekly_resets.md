Anthropic's `GET /api/oauth/usage` endpoint has at times returned `resets_at: null` for the `seven_day` bucket, often for `five_hour` with it, until `resets_at` came back. Across June–October 2026 all 17 such runs began at a 7d reset (scheduled or mid-window) and none followed a 5h event alone, but not every reset had one: 17 of 29 did, and none of the scheduled resets from 09-13 on. The null `resets_at` is the transient; the weekly zero is not (see below).

Observed 2026-07-01: `five_hour_pct` climbed to 100% (~13:36 local), both buckets flipped to `0.0` / `null` at ~14:16 and stayed there ~36 min (across a redeploy), then a direct query returned `five_hour: 2%` (real reset ~07-02 02:50) / `seven_day: 0%` (real reset ~07-06 03:00). `LimitBar` faithfully renders `utilization:0` + `resets_at:null` under status `ok` as `0% … IDLE` with an empty track — so **empty/IDLE bars appearing right after a limit maxes out come from upstream data, not a dashboard bug; don't re-investigate them.** They correct themselves only when the endpoint returns `resets_at` again, which has taken from under a minute to most of a day, so they can outlast many polls; re-showing the widget polls at once (mount calls `refreshUsageLimits`) but cannot end the transient sooner. Note the stale-refresh effect in `LimitBar.svelte` bails on `resets_at === null`, so it won't proactively re-poll while the zeros are held.

The 7d zero on 07-01 is **not** an artifact, and it is not a product of the 5h cap either. The 7d bucket dropped 51%→0% within the same weekly window (`seven_day_resets_at` unchanged), and across June–October 2026 every such drop stayed low afterwards (checked 2026-10-15: max 0–12% in the next 3h, never back to the prior level) — the weekly usage really was reset mid-window, ten times in that span (06-09, 06-12, 06-18, 07-01, 07-09, 07-15, 08-27, 09-01, 09-04, 10-02). Those resets are independent of the 5h bucket: the peak `five_hour_pct` in the 6h before each was 93, 7, 21, 100, 96, 39, 100, 19, 11 and 40, so only 07-01 and 08-27 followed a 5h cap. Don't look for a 5h cap to explain or predict a weekly reset, and don't dismiss one that had none before it. `usage_history::seven_day_resets` (the Work intensity chart's reset lines) therefore counts a reading ≤5% that is at least 10 points under the one before it as a reset, beside the `resets_at` jump; `build_weekly_quota`'s clamp already treated it as one.

The **limit-reset Telegram ping** (`notifications.rs` `ResetTracker`, added 2026-07-04) never treats a bare pct drop as a reset. Its prompt path fires when `resets_at` clears to null with the pct cleared *and* that window's scheduled `resets_at` has arrived (`RESET_ARRIVAL_SLACK_MS`); its fallback is the `resets_at` forward jump (> a 10-min margin). A mid-window 7d reset fails the arrival gate (its scheduled reset is days away) and recovers to the same `resets_at`, so the ping misses those — known, not changed. Its `peak_pct` is a running `max()`, so across a mid-window 7d reset it keeps the spent window's peak and the next scheduled reset's "was N%" can report that — known, not changed. Do not "unify" it with `build_weekly_quota`'s pct-drop detector — the two intentionally use opposite signals for opposite reasons (the roll-up wants a pct drop clamped to 0; the ping wants an arrived scheduled reset, and so misses mid-window resets).
